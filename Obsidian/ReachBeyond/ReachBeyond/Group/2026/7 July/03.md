# Homework 2026 July 03 - Blame

- **What belief did I notice in myself?**

- I use blame as an avoidance self-protection mechanism. I use it co-dependently taking responsibility even when it isn't and/or couldn't be my fault. I don't blame others, I blame myself. It doesn't matter how we got to where we are, it was my fault. I do this to avoid dealing with the situation. If there's someone to blame, it feels over. It also takes any unwanted feelings for responsibility for getting into a difficult situation onto myself so that I am the only one that has to feel bad about it.

- **When do I tend to blame?**

- I have often blamed myself when someone is getting mad, frustrated, or otherwise expressing distress or discomfort to try to minimize the situation[^1] and try to avoid potential conflict.

- **What would accountability look like instead?**

- Focusing on the solution. Blame isn't a tool to solve problems or make things better, and it also only arises when we want to take something out on someone. 

1. Identify the problem objectively.
2. Solve the current problem.
3. Determine how the problem occurred

- **What is one healthier belief I want to practice?**

- Proactive accountability and solving problems respectfully. Blame doesn't solve a problem and in my life sits in the same place as leaving a situation - it is to deflect long enough to not deal with the situation.

  

The only trip I planned out was to Mt. Rainier one summer for the family. It was a small walk about 0.5 miles. I didn't have anything else planned. I thought it would take a lot longer than it did, I didn't have perspective on how long it would take. It took about 15 minutes. Instead of attempting to recover the day and do something else, I said "it's all my fault" which just made me feel bad and completely unhelpful. The day was salvaged and we went on a longer tour of the area, but it wasn't because of me, I was ready to pack up and go home.

Things I can do better next time:

- Communication

- Work with the team to confirm the plan before we go.

- Focus on the solution.

- What can we do to enjoy the area more.

- Come up with a plan to avoid the scenario that seemed problematic

- Talk about what went wrong, what assumptions I made wrong, and how we will work through it next time.

  

When I was a child, I used blame for attention. I still remember it being self-blaming. When I was on the bus one day, the seat had been destroyed by someone with a knife. When the driver off-handedly asked back who had done it, I yelled up that I had done it. I was suspended from the bus and my family had to pay back the repair costs. I didn't know it at the time I was asked, and when asked why I did it, I shrugged and sheepishly said "I don't know", but I was doing it for attention then.
